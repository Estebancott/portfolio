# Renovation and Modernization of a Caravan

It all started when my father bought an **IMV Adria Prima 4152 D caravan** to install at our pond.
The idea was simple: to be able to sleep there when we have guests, or just enjoy a shelter during our fishing afternoons and small evening gatherings.

My little brother and my father quickly tackled the **restoration** of the caravan, as it had suffered water infiltration and had mold.
While they were busy fixing up the interior, I thought to myself: *"Why not take advantage of the work to add a real electrical and audio system?"*

---
## The Initial Need
Until now, when we wanted music and light at the pond, we brought a fully charged **tractor battery**, to which we connected a speaker and some lamps.
Practical but restrictive, since we always had to transport and prepare the equipment.

There was already a small shed equipped with a **solar panel**, but only to provide a little light.
No fridge, no music, no phone charging.

The idea was therefore to make the caravan **energy independent**, with all necessary comforts on site.

---

## Understanding the Original Electrical Circuit
The caravan was initially equipped with a **220 V circuit**, protected by a circuit breaker, powering a **220 V / 12 V transformer**.
This allowed powering the lighting, the refrigerator, the water pump, and the heating ventilation.

> **Excerpt from the user manual:**
> "The electrical circuit
> Your caravan is equipped as standard with a 220 V electrical circuit, protected by a circuit breaker with 16 A cartridge fuses which powers domestic power outlets. This installation allows you to plug in electrical appliances whose total power must not exceed 3,000 W. In addition, this circuit also powers a 220 V/12 V transformer, from 150 VA to 350 VA depending on the model, which thus makes available a 12 V low voltage circuit, substituting for the towing vehicle's 12 V circuit when you have the possibility of connecting your caravan to the electrical network.
> The 12 V circuit powers the interior lighting of your caravan, electric pumps, refrigerator, and heating ventilation.
> The 220 V power supply for your caravan comes from an electrical box located outside, connected to the circuit breaker and the transformer placed in one of the chests.
> The centralization of all circuits takes place in the multi-service combination placed above the kitchen unit.
> It is equipped with a built-in light fixture (12 V 25 W screw bulb) which allows complete lighting of the worktop and sink. In some models (SUPRA and MAXIMA range) it is equipped as standard with an electric hood which exhausts cooking vapors to the outside.
> Each circuit has its switch with indicator light and is protected by a fuse (see function table on page 24 and diagram opposite)."

## Audio System Design (Salvage)

For the audio part, I wanted to prioritize reuse. I recovered most of the components (speakers, car radio, cabling) from a car destined for the scrapyard. This allows for equipment natively designed for 12 V.

![Salvaged audio components](/projects/Caravane/composants.jpeg)
*Overview of components recovered for the audio system design*

I selected **6 speakers** to distribute sound evenly, as well as a classic car radio. To get cleaner sound, I worked on the filters to properly separate bass, mids, and highs.

---

## Electrical Installation and Power Management

One of the challenges was managing the energy source. Power can come from two sources:
1. The **battery** (autonomy at the pond, recharged by solar panel).
2. An **external socket** (220V mains).

To manage this, there is a **switching relay**.

![Electrical connections and relay](/projects/Caravane/branchements_autoradio.jpeg)
*System wiring with the priority relay*

This relay allows automatic switching between the two sources: the **external socket has priority**. As soon as it is plugged in, the relay cuts the power via the battery to preserve it. If no socket is plugged in, the system naturally switches to the battery.

I also integrated a small control module displaying battery voltage in real-time, coupled with USB ports for charging.

## Result and Integration

The integration was designed to be as discreet as possible. The 6 speakers were embedded directly into the existing furniture.

![Left side integration](/projects/Caravane/resultat_gauche.jpeg)

On the right side, we find the "dashboard" embedded in the furniture:
- The car radio.
- The voltmeter (to monitor the battery).
- The phone charger with integrated cable retractors.

![Right side integration](/projects/Caravane/resultat_droite.jpeg)
*Right side: Car radio, voltmeter, and chargers integrated into the furniture*

Today, the 1989 caravan has become a real **modern little refuge**:
- interior light powered by 12 V,
- music via Bluetooth thanks to the car radio,
- easy phone charging,
- autonomy thanks to the solar panel.

We can now fully enjoy the pond without having to transport all the equipment every time.
