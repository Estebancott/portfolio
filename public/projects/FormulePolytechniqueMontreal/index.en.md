## My Adventure at Montreal Polytechnic Formula

As part of my university exchange at **Polytechnique Montréal**, I had the immense luck of joining the **Montreal Polytechnic Formula** team. It is much more than a simple student association: it is a real racing team that designs and manufactures a 100% electric single-seater from A to Z.

The ultimate goal is to participate in **Formula SAE**, an extremely demanding international competition where universities from all over the world compete. To participate, strict specifications must be met, and the car is evaluated on numerous tests: endurance, acceleration, but also on technical design, cost management, and even the presentation of a business plan.

As an embedded systems enthusiast, joining the software team in this high-performance context was an incredibly formative experience.

### Project 1: BMS Firmware Porting (STM32F4 to L4)

My first mission was a major challenge: **porting the BMS (Battery Management System) firmware**. The electronics team had decided to change the board's microcontroller, moving from an **STM32F4** to an **STM32L4**, mainly for its much lower power consumption.

My role was therefore to take existing code, fully functional on the old architecture, and make it perfectly operational on the new one. It wasn't just a simple compilation: I had to dive into the code, understand interactions with peripherals (timers, CAN, GPIO), and adapt them to the specificities of the STM32L4. It was an excellent exercise in reverse engineering and low-level programming that required a lot of rigor.

---

### Project 2: Automation of Motec Data Analysis

The team was losing precious time after each driving session. We had to manually connect a PC to the car's **Motec** steering wheel, download the log files, and then analyze them one by one on proprietary software. The data often remained on a single computer, complicating sharing and collaborative analysis.

To solve this problem, I developed a script in **Python** that automates the whole process:
1.  **File Parsing**: The script uses an open-source parser to read proprietary Motec `.ld` files.
2.  **Conversion to CSV**: It then converts this raw data into a CSV format, which is much more universal.
3.  **Upload to Cloud**: Finally, it automatically sends these CSV files to **Marple**, an online data visualization platform.

Thanks to this tool, all session data is now centralized, accessible to all team members, and easily analyzable. We can give much faster feedback to the driver, for example by analyzing their braking curves to see if they can optimize their trajectories.

![Visualization of telemetry data on Marple](/projects/FormulePolytechniqueMontreal/viualisationSurMarple.png "Example of dashboard on Marple for post-race analysis.")

### Project 3: Battery 3D Heatmap

To help the mechanical team optimize battery cooling, I developed another tool in **Python**. By reusing the CSV data generated by my first script, I retrieve all information from the thermal sensors scattered throughout the battery.

In close collaboration with other departments to understand the exact location of each sensor, I created a script that generates an **interactive 3D heatmap**. We can thus visualize the temperature evolution of each cell over time, identify hot spots, and make decisions on the cooling system design.

![Real-time 3D battery heatmap](/projects/FormulePolytechniqueMontreal/heatmap.png "3D visualization of battery temperatures, with a slider to navigate through time.")

### Project 4: Improving Real-Time Telemetry Reliability

The last project I was able to lead before the end of my exchange was to **improve the reliability of real-time telemetry**. The existing system, based on **Xbee** modules, was not robust at all: we were losing more than 95% of sent messages!

To remedy this, I completely revised the communication protocol:
-   **Switch to API Mode**: I configured the Xbee modules to operate in API mode, which allows for structured data frames with checksums, much more reliable than the transparent mode used previously.
-   **Code Adaptation**: I modified the embedded code on the car and the receiver code on the PC so that they support this new protocol. I notably developed a frame parser on the PC side to reconstruct CAN messages, even if they arrive fragmented.

After these modifications, the tests were conclusive: over a 20-minute trial, **we went from 5% to 100% of messages received**. This completely changed the game for live feedback to the driver during tests.

## Summary of an Intense and Collaborative Experience

This experience in the Montreal Polytechnic Formula association was incredible. Working in such a large and structured team (nearly 50 people!) was highly enriching, especially compared to my experience at ENSIM'elec where we were 9. Communication and coordination between different departments (mechanical, electronic, software) are essential, and I loved this environment where every line of code has a direct impact on performance.

Even though I couldn't go all the way to the competition in Michigan, I am very proud of everything I was able to accomplish and contribute to the team in just 4 months, while taking my classes on the side.
